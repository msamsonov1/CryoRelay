Electrodynamic & Thermodynamic Physics of Cryogenic Latching

Multi-kiloampere DC switching in vacuum cryostats operates in extreme conditions where conventional contact lubrication and arc extinguishment fail. CryoRelay utilizes high-speed $(F, L)$ state projection to eliminate micro-welding at the source.

1. Magnetic Circuit Air-Gap & Inductance Coupling

The total inductance $L(g)$ of the actuator coil is governed by Hopkinson’s Law for magnetic circuits, inversely proportional to total circuit reluctance $\mathcal{R}_{\text{total}}$:

$$L(g) = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} + \mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}}(g)} = \frac{N^2}{\frac{l_c}{\mu_r \mu_0 A_c} + \frac{2 g(t)}{\mu_0 A_g}}$$

Where $N$ is turn count, $g(t)$ is the instantaneous armature vacuum air gap, $A_g$ is effective pole cross-sectional area, and $\mu_r \gg 10^3$ for high-saturation cryoperm alloys. As $g(t) \to 0$, $L$ spikes asymptotically. A freeze in the rate of change $\frac{dL}{dt}$ before reaching $L_{\text{closed}}$ proves mechanical impact rebound (chatter) before physical contacts achieve cold-welded conduction.

2. Cryogenic Micro-Welding & Holm's $\psi$-$\theta$ Equation

When contacts chatter under a 5.0 kA DC bus current, the contact constriction resistance $R_c$ dissipates localized Joule heat. In a vacuum with no convection, the bridge temperature $\theta_m$ is defined by Holm’s relation:

$$U_c^2 = 8 \int_{\theta_0}^{\theta_m} \frac{\kappa(T)}{\sigma(T)} \, dT \implies \theta_m \approx \sqrt{\theta_0^2 + \frac{U_c^2}{4 L_{\text{Lorenz}}}}$$

At $20\text{ K}$, thermal conductivity $\kappa(T)$ drops drastically for standard claddings while the Lorenz number $L_{\text{Lorenz}}$ dictates rapid runaway. If the constriction voltage $U_c$ exceeds $0.4\text{ V}$ during bounce separation, the asperities exceed the melting point ($1356\text{ K}$ for Cu), causing catastrophic vacuum micro-welds that permanently freeze the relay shut.

3. High-Speed Observer Inversion & Phase Trajectory

Rather than relying on fragile optical sensors inside the 20 K cryostat, the FPGA senses the instantaneous coil terminal response $v(t) = i R + \frac{d(L i)}{dt}$ driven by a $200\text{ kHz}$ low-amplitude pilot tone:

$$\hat{g}(t) = \frac{\mu_0 A_g}{2} \left( \frac{N^2}{L_{\text{meas}}(t)} - \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} \right), \quad \hat{F}_{\text{net}}(t) = m \frac{d^2 \hat{g}}{dt^2} + c \frac{d\hat{g}}{dt} + k \hat{g}$$

The observer projects the trajectory in the $(F, L)$ phase plane. If the trajectory crosses into the forbidden chatter corridor ($\Delta L < \epsilon_L$ while $F > F_{\text{seat}}$), the FPGA triggers the reverse damping coil within $18.2\text{ }\mu\text{s}$.

4. Superconducting Magnetic & Substrate Architecture

The relay combines a bi-stable permanent magnetic latch with dual high-speed electromagnetic coils wound from low-loss stabilized conductors:

$$F_{\text{hold}} = \frac{B_r^2 A_m}{2 \mu_0} \ge 420\text{ N}, \quad \tau_{\text{pulse}} = \frac{L_{\text{damp}}}{R_{\text{ext}}} \le 15.0\text{ }\mu\text{s}$$

The latching structure uses grade N52H NdFeB magnets with zero holding power consumption. The contact tips feature $50\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ electroplated Ag-In alloy asperities over high-purity oxygen-free copper, preventing diffusion bonding at sub-ambient temperatures.
